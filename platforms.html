Reach is rented.

Algorithms and marketplace traffic can change. Use them for discovery, but do not confuse reach with ownership.

Own the domain; export the content.

A home base is more controllable than a rented profile, but a site builder may not make every page or database portable.

Fees are only one cost.

Also count time, storage, fulfillment, customer data, lock-in, and the work required to stay visible.

Consistency beats presence everywhere.

One coherent identity and a reliable next step are more valuable than abandoned accounts on ten services.

Audience system

The public-facing path from first encounter to a meaningful next step.

Discovery→Website / home base→Relationship→Sale / booking

Asset system

The technical foundation that protects the work behind the public presentation.

Local master→Backup→Web export→Delivery / publish

Control and exit check

  • Account: Is the artist the owner, with two-factor authentication and recovery codes?
  • Domain: Is the domain registered in the artist’s own account?
  • Export: Can the artist retrieve files, contacts, subscribers, orders, and site content?
  • Terms: What rights does the service claim over uploaded work, data, or AI training?
  • Exit: What happens to the files, links, audience, and public pages if payment stops?

Relationship is an asset

An audience is not the same as a follower count. Treat consent-based email addresses, booking contacts, client records, and transaction history as exportable business records.

Use a professional email address and keep a current export outside the platform.

Platform means a job

In this guide, a platform may be a website builder, discovery channel, marketplace, file host, distributor, newsletter service, or payment layer. One service may do several jobs; no service should silently become the only archive.

Keep the layers distinct: Domain = web address · website builder = presentation · email = communication · file host = access · backup = recovery.
02 · Technical foundation

Local first; published second

Keep organized source files locally, back them up independently, and publish only deliberate exports.

Streaming is publishing, not archiving.
We are not assuming local hosting for everything. “Local first” means the artist has an organized source-of-truth library under their control. A website host, cloud storage service, digital store, or streaming platform can serve selected public or delivery copies. None of those should be the only archive.

Master files

Editable notation files, WAV/AIFF masters, RAW/TIFF/PSD images, high-resolution video, original design files, and complete project folders.

Web exports

Compressed audio, web-sized images, selected score excerpts, embedded video, and a current electronic press kit (EPK) or portfolio PDF.

Delivery copies

Full scores, parts, stems, press photos, tech riders, licenses, and client files shared through the right access level.

Composer website example

Work page: title, instrumentation, duration, year, program note, credits, recording, video, and a one- to three-page score excerpt that the artist has permission to publish.

Next step: “Request full score/parts” or “Commission this work.”

Performer website example

Electronic press kit (EPK) page: short and long bio, repertoire, press photos, performance video, calendar, press quotes, technical rider, and contact form.

Next step: “Book this performer” or “Download EPK.”

Public is not the only access level

Use public pages for discovery, restricted links for full materials, and paid delivery when the work is licensed or sold. “Contact me” is a call to action, not a substitute for evidence.

Public-facing baseline

Use alt text, captions, transcripts where useful, readable PDFs, descriptive links, and a professional contact email. Check the page on a phone before sending people there.

Rights gate

Before publishing, sharing, selling, or licensing a file, confirm permissions for collaborators, performers, samples, clients, publishers, models, locations, and commissioned material.

Export matrix

Keep master, web, review, and delivery versions distinct. The public file should be the smallest useful export, not the highest-value source.

Can I publish it?

Confirm permission to show the work, recording, image, excerpt, collaborator credit, sample, model, location, or client material in the place you selected.

What does the client receive?

State whether the client receives a license, ownership, or permission to perform, record, or synchronize the work; where it may be used, for how long, whether use is exclusive, and whether the artist may show it in a portfolio.

What travels with the file?

Keep credits, releases, collaborator splits, composition and sound-recording rights, catalog identifiers, and the delivery terms with the project record.

File audit

  1. Choose one work. Locate its score, recording, image, or video master.
  2. Organize and rename it. Put it in a project folder using the naming pattern above.
  3. Make an export. Create one web-ready or delivery-ready copy.
  4. Check permissions. Confirm that you can publish, share, sell, or license every component.
  5. Choose access. Mark the file public, link-only, invited/restricted, or password/expiring where supported. Record separately whether it is free review, paid delivery, or contract-bound. Do not call an unlisted link private.
  6. Back it up. Identify the local, second-storage, and off-site copies.
  7. Write the metadata. Record title, date, credits, rights, version, and the intended next step.
03 · File hosting and delivery

Share files without publishing them

Cloud storage is useful for internal collaboration, review, and delivery. Public websites and streaming services are for public presentation.

Default: restricted access
Restricted sharing is a separate job from public hosting. Start with specific people or a restricted folder, choose view/comment/edit access where the service offers it, and share a delivery copy—not the only master. “Anyone with the link” is effectively public.
Access modes: Public means discoverable; link-only means anyone with the link; invited/restricted means named people or accounts; password or expiring means the service provides an additional control. Working folder

For named collaborators. Use edit access only when people need to add, revise, or organize files.

Review or delivery link

For a commissioner, venue, client, or performer. Use view-only access, a password, or an expiration date when the service supports them.

Public page

For anyone to see. Upload web exports, selected excerpts, EPKs, and listening copies—not source files or non-public materials.

Simple delivery workflow

  1. Keep the master locally.
  2. Make a delivery copy. Add the version and intended recipient to the filename or delivery note.
  3. Upload to a restricted folder.
  4. Set the access level. View, comment, or edit—where the service offers those roles.
  5. Send the link. Record what was sent and when.
  6. Revoke or expire access when the project or review period ends.

Hosting is not backup

A synced cloud folder can mirror an accidental deletion or overwrite. Keep a separate backup copy, preserve versions when possible, and test restoration periodically.

Use cloud hosting for access and collaboration; use independent backups for recovery.

Before a project begins

Use an agreement or written scope covering deliverables, deadline, revisions, rights, payment terms, cancellation terms, and the file-access plan.

Count the whole cost

Subscription + domain/email + payment or marketplace fees + storage/backup + production/fulfillment + time.

Keep the record

Export invoices, receipts, contracts, permissions, delivery notes, and client or booking contacts outside the platform.

Simple project flow

Inquiry→Agree on scope→Create / perform→Deliver→Payment / record→Follow up

For a small project, an email or short written agreement may be enough. Payment may happen before or after delivery; keep the scope, deadline, rights, and payment terms together.

Not every exchange requires an invoice. It is a useful record of who paid, what the work was, when it happened, and what was delivered.

W-9 and 1099-NEC: For a U.S. person paid for independent-contractor work, a payer may ask for Form W-9 to collect a legal name and taxpayer identification number; give it to the requester, not the IRS. International students or non-U.S. payees may receive different tax paperwork. A W-9 concerns the payer/payee relationship, not whether payment arrives by cash, check, ACH, or platform. Use the payer’s secure submission method; never put a taxpayer ID in a public form or file. A payer may later issue Form 1099-NEC for reportable nonemployee compensation. A 1099-NEC is a tax information form—not an invoice and not a substitute for your own records. Compare it with your contracts and payment records. 07 · Take-home plan

A realistic 90-day platform plan

The point is to make one coherent system more professional and more useful.

Choose an identity.

Settle on the artist name, spelling, profile image, short biography, and consistent contact email.

Secure the accounts.

Own the domain and platform accounts, turn on two-factor authentication, store recovery codes, and use a password manager.

Organize the source library.

Gather one representative work, separate master files from web exports, use stable filenames, and record credits and rights.

Make the work recoverable.

Use the 3–2–1 rule: three copies, two storage media or destinations, and one off-site copy. Test that a file can actually be restored.

Build the minimum home base.

Publish three to five strong examples, a clear “about” statement, credits, accessible contact information, and one obvious next step.

Select one discovery channel.

Choose the place where the artist can realistically publish process, excerpts, performance, or project context.

Create a repeatable relationship.

Start an email list, newsletter, membership, or direct-support channel with a specific benefit for subscribers.

Launch one paid, booking, or commissioning path.

Choose one commission, print, download, release, booking service, lesson, workshop, or membership tier—and document its scope, rights, and payment terms.
