Marketing, Brand & Story

Movement IV: marketing, brand, outreach

Movement IV turns to how your work reaches people. It starts with the story that holds it together: the through-line you can carry across every context.

reading · Kolb 8, NYFA 11, Rabideau 10, Andrews 3 reflection due · Mon Nov 2, before class component due · Fri Nov 6, Canvas

this week

A brand is not a logo. It is the story and the values people attach to your work, and the consistency with which you hold them. Kolb and NYFA both land there: a brand is the experience a name calls up, and its whole job is clarity and consistency, which is exactly why many artists resist the word.

Rabideau supplies the spine. Under any brand is a story, and its core answers three things a stranger wants to know: why you do this (your origin, your heart), what makes you different, and where you would take someone who comes along. He compresses those into a single sentence he calls a hero declaration, and his point is practical: build everything from that one line and your message stays consistent whether it is a bio, a grant, a post, or a stage introduction. That consistency is the through-line.

Andrews is the reach: marketing is how the story finds an audience and educates them into an informed choice, through the materials that carry your image outward. Read for your own through-line, the thread that runs through everything you make. The reflection asks you to tell the story of your work in a single paragraph, the one a stranger would actually need, and then to name that thread.

the reading

Two new chapters and two revisited for their brand sections. Focus where noted.

Kolb, ch. 8, "Promoting the creative product" (pp. 175–197)

Brand as image and values: a brand expresses what the consumer cares about, carries your mission, and can rest on the story of the creator.

look for · the point that a brand image communicates an intangible experience, and the life story of the artist as a brand.

NYFA, ch. 11, "Marketing Principles and Strategies" (pp. 167–181)

Read the "Branding" section: a brand as a customer experience, and clarity and consistency as its whole point.

look for · why brands aim for clarity, and why artists resist calling their work a brand.

Rabideau, ch. 10, "Getting the Word Out: Storytelling, Marketing, and Communications Strategy" (pp. 115–132)

The story spine: what a brand story must answer, and the hero declaration that compresses it into one line held consistent across platforms.

look for · the three things a stranger wants to know, and the hero declaration built from them.

Andrews, ch. 3, "Marketing" (pp. 67–105)

Read the image-and-audience opening: how marketing builds and spreads your image, and educates an audience into an informed choice.

look for · marketing as shaping and disseminating your image, and the materials that tell the story behind your vision.

portfolio component due Fri Nov 6 · Canvas

Brand Narrative

A Brand Narrative is the through-line of your work told as a coherent story, one you can carry across contexts, a bio, a grant, a website, a stage introduction, without it changing shape. It answers why you do this, what makes your work distinct, and where it takes people, and it stays recognizable wherever it appears.

Questions to build it from:

  • Why do you do this work: your origin, the reason under it?
  • What is the through-line, the thread that runs through everything you make?
  • What makes your work distinct, drawn from your positioning?
  • Where does your work take the people who engage with it?
  • Can the story hold its shape across a bio, a grant, a post, and a spoken introduction?
