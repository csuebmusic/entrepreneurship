Product, Practice & Offerings

Module II: audience, positioning, product

The last two weeks located your audience and your position. This week names what you actually offer them, and describes it as value rather than a list of things you do.

this week

An offering is any distinct thing your practice provides: something you make, perform, teach, or host. All three readings turn on one distinction about how to describe it. Landry puts it plainest: a feature is what something is, a benefit is what it does, and buyers care about the benefit, the feeling, use, or identity your work gives them, not the activity that produces it.

Kolb builds that into what he calls a bundle of benefits: a core benefit (the need your offering meets), the actual thing (its attributes), and an augmented layer around it (the experience of getting it). He adds that creative work is distinctive rather than commoditized, so it competes on value, not price. NYFA starts a step earlier, with understanding your own work: what you do, and what makes it uniquely yours, before you frame it for anyone. Notice that in the arts an offering is often a service or an experience rather than an object, since it happens in real time.

Read for the gap between what you do and what it is worth to someone. It is easy to describe a practice as a list of activities: I compose, I perform, I teach. Harder, and the point of this week, is to say what each of those gives the person on the other end. Your practice likely produces several offerings, so part of the work is naming which one carries the most value for the audience you profiled.

portfolio component due Fri Oct 16 · Canvas

Product or Service Description

A Product or Service Description says what you offer, framed as value rather than a list of activities. It names one offering (a product, a service, or an experience) and describes what it gives the audience you have profiled: the benefit, not just the feature. It should sit consistently with your Positioning Statement.

Questions to build it from:

  • What are the offerings your practice produces, and which one are you describing here?
  • Is it a product, a service, or an experience, and what form does it take?
  • What is its core benefit: the need or want it meets for your audience?
  • What does it give the person on the other end, a feeling, a use, an identity, beyond the activity that makes it?
  • Why is that value distinct, given your positioning?

Write a Product or Service Description of one offering: what it is (product, service, or experience), and what value it gives the audience you profiled. Frame it as benefit rather than a list of activities, and keep it consistent with your Positioning Statement. Submit it on Canvas by 11:59 pm Friday.
