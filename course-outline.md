# course outline and schedule

Fall 2026, MUS 603 (graduate). Online synchronous, Mondays 6–9pm by Zoom. Term August 18 to December 5, 2026; finals December 7–12. This file locks the topical arc, the weekly schedule, the reading assignments, and the two weekly instruments with their per-week prompts. Refinements still in flux are listed under "still open."

## calendar and delivery

Thirteen live Mondays, two asynchronous campus-closure weeks (Labor Day, September 7; Fall Recess, November 23), and finals week. Fifteen content slots feed the portfolio, then the final. The live Monday is the seminar core: reading discussion, case analysis, and peer workshop of portfolio components in progress. Async work surrounds it, with readings completed before class, the weekly Activity A, and the weekly Portfolio Builder.

## reading core

Kolb, NYFA, and Rabideau carry nearly every week and are the required texts. Andrews, Landry, and McCurdy are the rotation shorter chapters are pulled from. Branagan is removed from consideration. The graduate-seminar model drives the reading load: three to four shorter readings per week pulled across the shelf, rather than one text per week. Chapter numbers in the schedule are each book's own; "TK" is a Rabideau Toolkit how-to and "Ep" his Epilogue.

- Kolb, *Entrepreneurship in the Arts* (3rd ed.)
- NYFA, *The Profitable Artist*
- Rabideau, *Creating the Revolutionary Artist*
- Andrews, *Arts Entrepreneurship*
- Landry, *Applying Entrepreneurship to the Arts*
- McCurdy, Goodstein & Lapin, *The Artist Entrepreneur*

## the two instruments

Two weekly instruments, distinct in purpose.

Activity A is the thinking. One to two pages at graduate level, written before Monday and used to seed discussion. Each makes two moves: engage the week's readings with analytical bite rather than summary, and connect that to a concrete question or decision in the student's own practice. It is the lower-stakes place to test ideas and bring something to workshop, and it primes the Builder that follows. The power and identity lens is a standing question any week's Activity A can pick up. It is not a keeper: it feeds the seminar and the component, then its job is done.

Portfolio Builder is the building. It produces one named component, a working professional artifact drafted that week, workshopped in seminar, and revised into the final portfolio. Length varies by component: a Mission Statement is a few lines, an Audience Profile or Business Model Snapshot runs longer. At 603 the marketing components carry an implemented step and accumulate into the marketing plan SLO 4 asks students to develop and implement, presented at the end.

The weekly rhythm is read, then Activity A (think and connect, before class), then seminar (discuss and workshop), then Portfolio Builder (produce the component). Fourteen weeks carry a Builder (week 3 produces two), for fifteen components. Week 13 runs Activity A only: there the reflective and analytical work is the whole point and no component is produced.

## activities and portfolio builders

Per week: the Activity A prompt, then the Portfolio Builder component and what it is. Prompts are seeds, refined into the Canvas assignment text later.

**I. Orientation, identity, and the power lens**

**week 1.** Activity A: what "entrepreneurship" means for an artist in the readings, where you locate yourself in that picture now, and one assumption the readings challenge. Builder, Creative Identity Statement: a first-person statement of who you are as an artist and the work you do, the anchor the rest of the portfolio refers back to.

**week 2.** Activity A: two or three values that actually govern your creative decisions, with a real example of each. Builder, Values Statement: the values that guide your choices, stated so they could guide an actual decision.

**week 3 (async).** Activity A: draft a one-sentence mission and a one-sentence vision, and note where they pull against each other. Builder, Mission Statement and Vision Statement: mission (what you do, for whom, why, now) and vision (the change you work toward).

**week 4.** Activity A: map the ecosystem around one path you are considering, its roles, gatekeepers, and organizations, and where power sits. Builder, Career Ecosystem Map: the people, organizations, venues, and revenue points around your path, and where you fit.

**week 5.** Activity A: how power and social identity have shaped access and opportunity in your discipline, drawing on Rabideau and the outside readings. Builder, Positionality and Access Reflection: your own position in the field, the access and barriers it carries, and what that means for the framework you are building. A durable anchor weeks 6 through 15 can refer back to.

**II. Audience, positioning, product**

**week 6.** Activity A: the audiences the readings say you actually have versus the one you imagine, and what you do not yet know about them. Builder, Audience Profile: who your audience is, what they value, where they are, and how they find work like yours. Opens the marketing plan.

**week 7.** Activity A: position yourself against two or three others working near you, and to whom that difference matters. Builder, Positioning Statement: the distinct value you offer and to whom, relative to the field.

**week 8.** Activity A: name the offerings your practice produces and which carries the most value for your audience. Builder, Product or Service Description: what you offer, framed as value rather than a list of activities.

**III. Business model and money**

**week 9.** Activity A: sketch how your practice makes or could make money across more than one stream, and where it is fragile. Builder, Business Model Snapshot: how your practice creates, delivers, and captures value, on a one-page canvas.

**week 10.** Activity A: price one offering three ways (cost, market, value) and defend one. Builder, Pricing Rationale: your pricing for at least one offering with the reasoning, including access considerations such as sliding scale.

**IV. Marketing, brand, outreach**

**week 11.** Activity A: tell the story of your work in a paragraph a stranger would need, and name the through-line. Builder, Brand Narrative: that through-line as a coherent story you can carry across contexts.

**week 12.** Activity A: audit where your audience actually is against where you actually show up, and name the gap. Builder, Outreach and Platform Strategy: the platforms and relationships you will use and a concrete plan. Where "implement" bites and the marketing plan closes.

**V. Legal and financial sustainability**

**week 13 (Activity A only).** Activity A: work an ethics-and-legal case from the readings (contracts, rights, IP, or a conduct scenario), how you would handle it, and what you would need to know. No component this week.

**week 14 (async).** Activity A: what sustaining your practice for a year would take, and where funding could come from. Builder, Financial Snapshot: the money in and out of your practice and a funding path fitted to it.

**VI. Impact, integration, futures**

**week 15.** Activity A: the societal impact you want your practice to have and one concrete way to pursue it. Builder, Portfolio Overview Statement: a short integrative introduction naming the through-line across the components, so the portfolio reads as one document.

**finals.** Compile, revise for coherence, and present. Not a weekly Builder.

## what's locked

Async placement went to the two most independent-friendly topics: mission and vision (week 3), which is template-driven and follows the identity weeks, and financial sustainability (week 14), dense how-to material that reads well as independent work.

The two outcomes with the thinnest reading base each get a dedicated session. 603-1 (power and social identity) anchors week 5; 603-5 (societal impact and community benefit) anchors week 15. The lens is also named at weeks 1 and 4 so it runs as a thread. Week 5 is the one week the shelf cannot carry alone: Rabideau 7 is the only chapter that treats the subject, so that week needs roughly three outside scholarly or industry readings, sourced before publishing.

The marketing-plan build path for 603 SLO 4 accumulates through weeks 6, 7, 11, and 12, then moves to implemented form and the synchronous presentation across week 15 and finals.

The portfolio spine holds fifteen components. Fourteen weeks carry a Builder (week 3 produces two, mission and vision); week 13 is the one Activity-A-only week. Week 5 leaves a durable Positionality and Access Reflection, so the two thinnest and most defining outcomes both leave a keeper. Fundraising folds into week 14 (Rabideau 11, NYFA funding chapters) rather than taking its own week.
