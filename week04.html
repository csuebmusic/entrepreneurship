Career Paths & Arts Ecosystems

Module I: orientation, identity, and the power lens

this week

An arts ecosystem is the whole field around a career: the collaborators, presenters, funders, venues, and audiences that a path runs through. McCurdy's self-management chapter is the most concrete about it. Without a manager, you are your own agent, and the field resolves into specific players: presenters who decide what gets booked, venues like college centers and museums, patrons, and the network you build. Andrews widens the view to the roles inside an organization, the trends reshaping the field, and the plain tools of a career, the résumé, the cover letter, the portfolio.

Both McCurdy's "creative space" chapter and Andrews say the same thing about the paths themselves: the old full-time track has thinned, so careers now run interdisciplinary and portfolio-shaped, built as much through relationships as through credentials. Hold onto McCurdy's blunt version of it, that technical skill may get you hired once, but relationships get you hired again.

The map is not neutral, and that is the part to read for. Presenters and funders are gatekeepers, and some paths are open to some people and closed to others. Rabideau's epilogue says it outright, in Stanford Thompson's work against racial inequality in classical music and in artists who bring their work to audiences that have been denied access. As you map your own field, mark where the power sits.

the reading

Four readings that come at the field from its structure, its economy, its concrete players, and its long arc. Read them in any order.

Andrews, ch. 8, "Organizational Design, Career Development, and Future Trends" (pp. 240–274)

The wide-angle view: how a venture is structured into roles, the concrete tools of a career (résumé, cover letter, portfolio, references), and the trends reshaping the field, from social-benefit organizations to creative placemaking.

look for · the Career Development section's job-market tools, and the Future Trends section's picture of where the field is heading.

McCurdy, ch. 3, "Finding Your Creative Space"

The changed arts economy and how to find room in it. McCurdy is blunt about the thinned job market and argues that your edge comes from a distinctive voice, from relationships, and from creative, often interdisciplinary, thinking.

look for · the point that relationships, not just technical skill, get you hired again, and the interdisciplinary and alternative paths he names.

McCurdy, ch. 6, "The Emerging Artist and Self-Management"

The most concrete map of the field: as your own agent, you deal with presenters, venues (college centers, libraries, museums), patrons, and your own network. This is the ecosystem in specifics.

look for · presenters as the gatekeepers, the range of venues, and "know your value" when you approach them.

Rabideau, Epilogue, "From Single Project to Rewarding Career" (pp. 147–152)

The long arc: how a single project becomes a career, through leadership, community, and access. It names the access question directly.

look for · the leaders who bring their work to audiences that have been denied access, and Stanford Thompson on racial inequality in classical music.

portfolio component due Fri Sep 11 · Canvas

Career Ecosystem Map

A Career Ecosystem Map is a picture of the field around one path you are considering: the people, organizations, venues, and revenue points that make it up, and where you fit among them. It can be an actual diagram or a structured, annotated list. Unlike the statements so far, this component looks outward, at the terrain rather than at you.

Questions to build it from:

  • What path are you mapping, and who are the people and organizations in it: collaborators, presenters, funders, audiences, employers?
  • Where are the gatekeepers, the ones who decide what gets booked, funded, or shown?
  • Where does the money come from, the revenue points along this path?
  • Where do you sit in this map now, and where do you want to move?
  • Where does power sit, and how does access to this path get distributed?

Map the ecosystem around your chosen path, as a diagram or an annotated list. Include the key people, organizations, venues, and revenue points; mark the gatekeepers and where power sits; and locate yourself in it. Submit on Canvas by 11:59 pm Friday.
