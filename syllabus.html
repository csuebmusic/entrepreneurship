description

Graduate-level course explores entrepreneurship in the arts and music industry. Analyze power dynamics, business aspects, and craft personal branding. Develop a comprehensive marketing plan, reflecting on arts' broader societal impact.

learning outcomes

Upon successful completion of this course students will be able to:

  1. Analyze the impact of power and social identity on career options in various arts disciplines.
  2. Navigate the logistical and business aspects of arts industries, drawing inspiration from historical and contemporary perspectives.
  3. Identify and articulate personal career paths in the arts, employing tools such as résumés, cover letters, and online media.
  4. Develop and implement a comprehensive marketing plan tailored to achieve success in a chosen career path.
  5. Demonstrate an understanding of the societal impact of arts and humanities perspectives, using this knowledge for the benefit of local and global communities.

course work

In place of a final exam, the course builds toward a professional portfolio. Each week carries two kinds of work: a reflection, and in most weeks a portfolio component.

reflections

A Reflection is a short piece, one to two pages, due before that week's Monday session. Each engages the week's reading and connects it to a decision in your own practice, and it seeds the seminar discussion and, in most weeks, the component that follows. Reflections are where you test ideas and bring something to workshop; they are not part of the final portfolio. Most weeks have one. The asynchronous week on career paths and ecosystems carries three portfolio components and no reflection, and the week on legal and professional issues is given to reflection and discussion alone.

portfolio components

A Portfolio Component is a working piece of your career framework, drafted that week, workshopped in seminar, and revised as the term goes on: who you are as an artist, who your audience is, how you position and price your work, how you reach people, and how you keep the practice going. Most weeks you add one, for seventeen in all, and each is due the following Friday, by 11:59 pm, on Canvas, so you build it after the seminar discussion. Two of them are the documents the field asks for directly, a résumé and a cover letter.

the portfolio

The portfolio is the seventeen components pulled together at the end, revised for clarity and voice so they read as one coherent document rather than a stack of worksheets. The pieces should fit: your Career Ecosystem Map should make sense given your Mission Statement, your Audience Profile should line up with your Positioning Statement, and so on. You present the finished portfolio in a synchronous session during the last week and finals week.

For format, a single organized PDF is recommended; a clearly labeled folder of documents (a ZIP, or a shared Drive link with viewing enabled) or a simple website (Google Sites, Notion, Adobe Portfolio) also work. The portfolio values clarity, integration, and professional awareness over polish. It shows where you are now, honestly and coherently.

use of AI

Generative AI (ChatGPT, Claude, Copilot, Gemini, and similar tools) is permitted in this seminar for two bounded purposes: checking grammar and mechanics in writing you have already drafted, and gathering preliminary background on an industry, market, or organization that you then verify against primary sources before you rely on it. Everything else is off limits. The tools may not generate your reflective and professional content. Your creative identity, values, positioning, marketing decisions, and the portfolio's own prose are yours, written in your own voice. The portfolio is a record of your thinking about your own practice, and that thinking does not transfer.

Every use of AI, including the two permitted ones, must be disclosed. Undisclosed use, or use beyond the two permitted purposes, will be treated as a violation of the CSUEB Academic Dishonesty Policy and may be subject to disciplinary action. This is the seminar's Generative AI use policy under CSUEB policy 25-26 CIC 1. For CSU guidance on the ethical and responsible use of AI, see the CSU AI guidance for students.

A caution specific to our work: these tools state facts, figures, and details about real artists and organizations that are wrong or invented. The case studies in your portfolio rest on real ventures, so verify every factual claim against an identifiable source. Treat AI output as a prompt to check your work, not as an authority.

policies

This is a synchronous seminar, and attendance and active participation each Monday are expected: discussion of the reading, case analysis, and workshop of components in progress. The two asynchronous weeks are completed independently by the posted deadline. If an emergency arises, email me as soon as possible.
