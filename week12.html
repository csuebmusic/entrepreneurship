Social Media, Platforms & Relationships

Module IV: marketing, brand, outreach

The marketing work of the last few weeks now has to reach someone. This week you turn it into a concrete plan: which platforms, which relationships, and what you will actually do.

reading · Kolb 9, NYFA 12, McCurdy 9, Rabideau Toolkit reflection due · Mon Nov 2, before class component due · Fri Nov 6, Canvas

this week

The first thing all four readings correct is the idea that reaching people means broadcasting at them. Kolb splits your presence into three kinds: paid media (ads you buy and control), owned media (your own site, feed, and mailing list), and earned media (other people talking about you, which you do not control). Owned media is where relationships are built, because it is two-way: you post, people respond, and you respond back. McCurdy puts it plainly, engagement matters more than follower counts, and a fan base grows from your close circle outward, one gathered email at a time.

You do not need to be everywhere. Each platform has its own culture and its own audience, so the useful move is to be where the people you profiled already are rather than on every platform at once. NYFA reviews the platforms, though its numbers are from 2016, so read it for the fit-to-audience logic and not the statistics. Rabideau's toolkit is the most concrete: a social media campaign is a plan with goals, chosen platforms, a posting schedule, someone responsible for it, and a way to measure whether it worked.

This is where the marketing plan stops being a framework and becomes something you do. Start from the honest audit the reflection asks for: where is your audience actually, and where do you actually show up? Name the gap. The strategy you build then has one job, to close it, with real platforms, real relationships, and steps concrete enough to act on this month.

the reading

Four readings from broadcast to relationship to concrete plan. All four are worth reading in full, though the Rabideau toolkit is short.

Kolb, ch. 9, "Building social media relationships" (pp. 198–222)

Social media as relationships, not broadcast: the paid, owned, and earned distinction, and why owned media (your own two-way channels) builds community.

look for · the three kinds of media, and why owned media takes more effort but builds more trust.

NYFA, ch. 12, "Marketing Tools and Resources" (pp. 182–205)

The practical toolkit, platform by platform. Read it for how to match a platform to your audience; the platform statistics are from 2016 and out of date.

look for · the idea that each platform has its own culture and audience, so you go where yours is.

McCurdy, ch. 9, "Marketing and the Use of Social Media"

Platform and plan built together, and building a fan base from your close circle outward, gathering contacts at every turn.

look for · the point that engagement beats follower counts, and the role of the mailing list.

Rabideau, Toolkit, "Social Media Campaign / Video Profile / Website" (pp. 184–189)

The step-by-step how-tos: a social media campaign with goals, platforms, a schedule, and measurement, plus a video profile and a website.

look for · the elements of a concrete campaign plan you can reuse for your own.

portfolio component due Fri Nov 6 · Canvas

Outreach and Platform Strategy

An Outreach and Platform Strategy names the platforms and relationships you will use to reach your audience, and lays out a concrete plan for doing it. This is the point where the marketing work of the last several weeks becomes something you can enact, so keep it specific enough to act on.

Questions to build it from:

  • Where does your audience actually spend time, and which platforms or channels reach them?
  • Which of those will you actually use, and which will you leave alone?
  • What relationships and owned channels (a mailing list, a community, key contacts) will you build or use?
  • What is the concrete plan: goals, a posting or outreach schedule, who does it, and how you will know it is working?
  • How does this close the gap between where your audience is and where you show up?
