Competition, Positioning & Value

Module II: audience, positioning, product

Last week drew your audience. This week asks where you sit among everyone else trying to reach a similar audience, and what you offer that they do not.

this week

Positioning is the distinct value your work offers, and to whom, relative to the field. You cannot name it without knowing that field. Kolb's chapter is about researching competitors, and his definition of a competitor is the one to hold: not only people making what you make, but anyone offering a similar benefit to the same audience. He argues for researching widely, since the distinctions you see between your work and theirs may be invisible to the people you are both trying to reach.

Andrews frames the same work as competitive analysis, usually done by studying the organizations near you, which in the arts can mean a collaborator as much as a rival. Landry supplies the inward half: your unique value, what you offer that the others do not, which he ties back to the identity and mission you named in Module I. NYFA calls the result your place in the market, and treats branding as the clarity and consistency with which you hold that place.

Read for the difference that matters, not every difference. It is easy to list ways you are unlike others. It is harder to name the one difference an actual audience would care about and choose you for. Positioning lives at that intersection, a real distinction and someone to whom it matters. It rests on last week's Audience Profile, and the two should agree.

Positioning Statement

A Positioning Statement names the distinct value your work offers, to whom, relative to the field. It is one or two sentences that say what you offer, who it is for, and how it differs from the alternatives your audience could choose instead. It rests on your Audience Profile and on the unique value you have been naming since Module I.

Questions to build it from:

  • Who is your work for, from your Audience Profile, and what alternatives could they choose instead?
  • Who or what are you positioned against: two or three others offering a similar benefit to that audience?
  • What is the distinct value you offer that they do not?
  • Why would this audience care about that difference, enough to choose you?
  • Can you say it in a sentence or two, in plain language rather than slogan?
